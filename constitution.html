    Chapter I: Foundation

    Article 1: Name, status and definitions

    1.1 The name of this organization is the International Hand Cricket Council, called "IHCC" in this Constitution. This document is called "The IHCC Constitution".

    1.2 IHCC is a non-governmental organization. It is not registered with any government authority. It exists for sport and entertainment. It has no connection with, and is not sponsored or approved by, the International Cricket Council, any national cricket board or any real cricketer. It is an organization for the sport and entertainment of blind players of Hand Cricket.

    1.2A The Board of Directors may decide, by more than two-thirds of all Directors, that IHCC be registered with a government authority. In that case the changes that registration needs are made under Article 30.

    1.3 IHCC governs Hand Cricket, a cricket game played on a mobile application by blind players, through national boards.

    1.4 In this Constitution:

    (a) "Member Board" means one of the national Hand Cricket boards that is a member of IHCC.

    (b) "Voting Member" means one of the first twelve players of a Member Board's squad.

    (c) "Voting List" means the list of all Voting Members, with their boards and Gmail addresses, prepared under Article 16.

    (d) "Board of Directors" means the body of the Team Directors of all Member Boards.

    (e) "Core Committee" means the six officers named in Article 14.

    (f) "Election Committee" means the committee of three members formed under Article 17 to conduct an election.

    (g) "More than half" means the smallest whole number greater than one half of the number concerned. For example, of twelve it is seven, and of thirteen it is also seven.

    (h) "More than two-thirds" means the smallest whole number greater than two-thirds of the number concerned. For example, of twelve it is nine, and of thirteen it is also nine.

    (i) "Main Group" means the IHCC group in which official notifications are issued.

    (j) "Official voting page" means the voting page on the official IHCC website.

    (k) "Rule Book" means the IHCC Rule Book made by the Board of Directors under Article 3, which holds the detailed rules.

    (l) "IHCC Management" means the temporary management that runs IHCC until the hand-over under Article 32.

    (m) "Badge" means the verification badge of a verified display name in the Hand Cricket application.

    (n) "Reserve account" means the account of IHCC in which badges are kept under clause 9.13.

    1.5 The numbers "more than half" and "more than two-thirds" are always worked out from the number of Member Boards, Directors or Voting Members at the time. Where a Director cannot vote on a matter because of an interest, the number is worked out from the Directors who can vote on it (for example, of eleven, more than two-thirds is eight).

    1.6 All dates and times in this Constitution are in Pakistan time.

    Article 2: Objects

    2.1 To promote and develop Hand Cricket among blind players around the world through an organized system of Member Boards.

    2.2 To keep justice, equality and fair play among Member Boards.

    2.3 To organize tournaments, series and world cups.

    2.4 To protect the trust and respect of players, including their verified identities.

    2.5 To run IHCC openly, honestly and with the advice of its members.

    2.6 To support deserving players, for example with premium or verification badges.

    Article 3: The Constitution and the Rule Book

    3.1 IHCC has two documents. This Constitution holds the main Articles. The IHCC Rule Book holds the detailed rules.

    3.2 An Article of this Constitution can be amended only under Article 30, by more than two-thirds of all Directors.

    3.3 The Board of Directors makes the Rule Book by more than half of all Directors. It holds the detailed rules of IHCC: the squad procedure, the playing conditions, match officials, tournaments and rankings, the code of conduct, offences and fines, the complaints procedure, the election rules, records, finance procedures and official communication.

    3.4 The Rule Book may not conflict with this Constitution. If a part of it does, this Constitution applies and that part has no effect.

    3.5 Where this Constitution says that a matter is decided in the Rule Book, that decision is binding on every member, officer and Member Board.

    3.6 The Rule Book is published on the official IHCC website and in the Main Group. A change takes effect when it is published, or on a later date that it states.

    3.7 Every Article, clause, Chapter and rule has a number, so that it can be referred to exactly. An Article of this Constitution is cited as "Article 11" or "clause 11.5". The Rule Book is cited as "Rule Book Chapter 7" or "Rule Book rule 7.3".

    Article 4: Languages and interpretation

    4.1 This Constitution is written in English. The English text is the original and the one to be followed.

    4.2 Translations in Hindi, Urdu and Roman Urdu may be made. They are for understanding only. If they differ from the English text, the English text applies.

    4.3 The headings of Chapters and Articles are for finding the place only, and do not change the meaning of a clause.

    4.4 A word in the masculine form includes the feminine, and a word in the singular includes the plural, unless the meaning is clear otherwise.

    4.5 "In writing" includes an email, and a form on the official IHCC website filled in after signing in with the member's own account. Where this Constitution allows the General Secretary to be told or asked "in any way", a message on WhatsApp is also enough.

    4.6 A word defined in clause 1.4 has the same meaning in the Rule Book.

    Chapter II: Members

    Article 5: Member Boards

    5.1 IHCC begins with twelve Member Boards, subject to Article 32. Each board has its own full name and a short code, as in this list:

    1. New Zealand Hand Cricket, NZHC

    2. England and Wales Hand Cricket Board, EWHCB

    3. Pakistan Hand Cricket Board, PHCB

    4. Bangladesh Hand Cricket Board, BHCB

    5. Afghanistan Hand Cricket Board, AHCB

    6. Hand Cricket Australia, HCA

    7. Sri Lanka Hand Cricket, SLHC

    8. Hand Cricket South Africa, HCSA

    9. Emirates Hand Cricket Board, EHCB

    10. USA Hand Cricket, USAHC

    11. Hand Cricket Association of Nepal, HCAN

    12. Board of Control for Hand Cricket in India, BCHCI

    5.2 Every Member Board has equal rights. There is no owner of a Member Board. A Member Board is governed by its players under this Constitution.

    5.3 A new Member Board is admitted only by the Board of Directors, by more than two-thirds of all Directors. The board must show: at least twelve real players; for each player a verified display name of a real cricketer of the country of the board (clause 8.3), confirmed by the General Secretary; a separate Gmail address and WhatsApp number for each player; and that no player is in the squad of another board. One country has one Member Board. The request is made in writing to the General Secretary with the squad list. The General Secretary checks within fourteen days and reports to the Directors.

    5.3A A new board is on trial for six months. In that time its Director votes on ordinary decisions (more than half), but his vote is not counted on decisions that need more than two-thirds: amending the Constitution, admitting or removing a board, removing a Core officer, and dissolution. Its Voting Members vote in elections. A board found to have a false or invented squad is suspended at once, and the Directors may remove it by more than two-thirds. After six months the Directors confirm the board by more than half. If they do not, the trial is extended once, for three months.

    5.4 A Member Board may leave under clause 5.4A. When a board leaves or is removed, its unpaid fees and its debts to IHCC are first set off against its credit. What remains is returned within thirty days, to an account that an officer of the board names in writing, in the officer's own name. The CFO makes a written account of the credit, the fees set off and the amount returned, the General Secretary records it, and the Main Group is told. IHCC may never keep the credit of a removed board as a sanction (clause 25.8). A fine is never set off against credit (clause 25.7). The badge prices of the players are a separate debt (clause 9.10). The badges stay with IHCC under clause 9.10.

    5.4A A Member Board leaves IHCC only if at least eight of its twelve Voting Members agree in writing. The Director alone cannot take the board out. The notice goes to the General Secretary with the names of the eight players, confirmed from their Gmail addresses, and runs for thirty days. Within twenty-four hours the General Secretary tells every player of the board (one to thirty) by email and WhatsApp. A player who does not wish to go with the board may move to another Member Board without the thirty days of clause 8.6 and without counting it as the one move in a year, if the new board accepts. A board may not leave from the closing of the Voting List until the result of the election, nor during a tournament in progress. A board that has left may not join again for ninety days, and then only as a new board (clauses 5.3 and 7.8).

    5.5 Boards not yet admitted are not Members, and no decision about them is taken by the Boards that already exist, except under clause 5.3.

    5.6 The country name of a board is for branding only. Players may be of any nationality.

    Article 6: Rights and duties of Member Boards

    6.1 Every Member Board has the right:

    (a) To have its Team Director on the Board of Directors, with one vote.

    (b) To have its Voting Members vote, and stand as candidates, in elections under this Constitution.

    (c) To be treated equally with every other Member Board.

    (d) To receive every official notice of IHCC.

    (e) To be heard before any sanction against it.

    (f) To take part in the tournaments and series of IHCC.

    6.2 Every Member Board has the duty:

    (a) To keep a squad of at least twelve players, as Article 8 requires.

    (b) To choose its Team Director and Deputy Director fairly under Article 11.

    (c) To pay the monthly fee under Article 22.

    (d) To follow this Constitution, the Rule Book and the decisions of IHCC.

    (e) To answer the questions of IHCC within a reasonable time.

    (f) To keep the personal data of its players safe under Article 10.

    (g) To respect the other boards and their players.

    (h) Not to stop, pressure or threaten a player who wishes to leave under clause 8.9A.

    Article 7: Suspension and removal of a Member Board

    7.1 Only the Board of Directors may suspend or remove a Member Board.

    7.2 Before it decides, IHCC gives the board the reasons in writing and a fair time to explain.

    7.3 A suspension lasts up to six months and is decided by more than half of all Directors. During it, the Director of the board does not vote and its members do not vote in elections. A suspension for unpaid fees is governed by clause 22.7. A suspension because the squad has stayed below twelve is governed by clause 8.1A.

    7.3A [Locked] When a Member Board is suspended because of its own fault (for example a false election report or unpaid fees), its players do not take part in the tournaments and series of IHCC, but their squad, names and badges stay. They do not vote in elections (clause 7.3). Any of its players may move to another Member Board without the thirty days of clause 8.6 and without counting it as the one move in a year, if the new board accepts. The suspended board does not register new players. The queue and payment of badge prices is not stopped. When the suspension ends, everything is as before, and a player who moved away in the meantime may return at once if a place is free.

    7.4 A Member Board is removed only by more than two-thirds of all Directors. The Director of the board concerned does not vote.

    7.5 A board may appeal once against a suspension or removal within seven days, under clause 25.9C.

    7.6 The credit of a removed board is dealt with under clause 5.4. The badges stay with IHCC under clause 9.10.

    7.7 A board disqualified under clause 32.3 for not submitting its squad is not covered by this Article.

    7.8 A board that has left or been removed can return only as a new board under clause 5.3.

    Article 8: Players, display names and squads

    8.1 Each Member Board has a squad of at least twelve and at most thirty players.

    8.1A [Locked] If a squad falls below twelve players, the board has thirty days to register new players. During this time the board's membership is not affected and its Director may vote. If the squad is still below twelve after thirty days, the board has one last period of fourteen days. If it is still below twelve after that, the board is suspended under clause 7.3, and the Director of that board does not vote on it. The General Secretary tells the board in writing how many players are missing and the last date. These periods stop running from the day the Voting List is closed until the result of the election concerned. The procedure is in the Rule Book.

    8.2 The first twelve players of the squad are Voting Members. They have the right to vote. The players from thirteen to thirty have all other rights of members, except the right to vote.

    8.2A A Member Board may play in a match any player from its whole squad, from one to thirty. It need not choose the first twelve. The board decides who plays.

    8.3 [Locked] The display name of every player is the verified name of a real cricketer of the country of the player's own board. A male player uses the name of a male cricketer. A female player uses the name of a female cricketer; but if she wishes, a female player may use the name of a male cricketer instead. The cricketer must have played at least one international match for that country, in T20, ODI or Test. One match is enough. A cricketer who has played only domestic cricket cannot be used. The display name must be verified in the Hand Cricket application, and it is written with the real name of the player. No other person may use a verified display name. [Locked] The General Secretary confirms, before a player joins a squad, that the display name belongs to such a cricketer and that it is verified in the Hand Cricket application. He may check this in any simple way he finds sufficient, and he records it.

    8.4 The real name of a player must be correct. The email address given for a player must be a Gmail address, and it is the address on which the player has the Hand Cricket premium. The same address is used for voting. [Locked] It is the player's own duty to keep the Hand Cricket premium of this address active. A player who does not do so is dealt with as a player who has disappeared under clause 8.9. [Locked] A player may change his or her Gmail address or WhatsApp number by asking the General Secretary, as the Rule Book says. From the day the Voting List is closed until the result of the election, the Gmail address of a Voting Member cannot be changed.

    8.5 A player may be in the squad of only one Member Board. If a player is found in two squads, the player must choose one board within forty-eight hours, and the other board will replace the player.

    8.6 [Locked] A player may move to another Member Board once in a year. The player gives written notice to the first board and to the General Secretary, and the new board accepts. The new board may refuse without giving a reason. The player joins the new squad only after thirty days from the date of the notice, and during these thirty days is in no squad. The player joins at the end of the squad, and may be moved up under clause 8.8. Leaving IHCC and returning under clause 8.6B counts in the same one move in a year.

    8.6A [Locked] When a player moves to another Member Board, the badge of the old display name goes to the reserve account under clause 9.9. The player must verify a new display name, of a cricketer of the country of the new board under clause 8.3, and must sell that new badge to IHCC under clause 9.4. The price of the new badge becomes a new debt of IHCC under clause 9.6, and is paid in its turn under clause 9.8. If the price of the old badge was not yet paid, that debt stays and is paid in its turn under clause 9.8, not within one week. Clause 9.9, which requires an unpaid price to be paid within one week, applies when a player leaves or is removed, and does not apply to a move to another Member Board. If the player does not verify the new name within the thirty days, the move is cancelled. He may go back to the old board only if a place is free there; otherwise he is treated as having left, and clause 8.6B applies.

    8.6B [Locked] A player who has left IHCC may join a squad again, of the same board or of another, only after ninety days from the date of leaving. A player removed under Article 25 may not join any squad until the sanction ends.

    8.7 From the day the Voting List is closed until the result of the election concerned, no change may be made to the first twelve players of a squad, and no transfer may take place. A Voting Member who leaves in this period keeps his name on the Voting List and may still vote. If he does not vote, he is counted as not having voted. He is replaced after the election under clause 8.9. In the same period new players may be added to a squad only at places thirteen to thirty.

    8.8 [Locked] Outside that period, a change of order among the players thirteen to thirty is made by the Team Director and the Deputy Director together, after consulting each other. A change that moves a player into or out of the first twelve is made by the Team Director, Deputy Director and Media Manager of the board, after advice with the squad and by the votes of more than half of the twelve Voting Members, that is, at least seven. The player to be moved from the Voting Members must first be allowed to speak, and does not vote on his own case. The board informs the General Secretary within twenty-four hours, with a written reason, and the General Secretary corrects the record. A player may not be moved down because he complained, appealed, voted or announced that he is leaving. The order is not changed more than once in three months, except to fill a place under clause 8.9. Any member may complain to IHCC, which will ask the other members and decide justly.

    8.9 [Locked] A Voting Member who resigns, dies, disappears, or is removed under Article 25 is replaced by the next player in the squad order. The same applies to any player of the squad: his place is taken by the next player. A player is treated as having disappeared when the Team Director has failed to reach him by WhatsApp and email for thirty days, the General Secretary has then sent a written notice to his registered Gmail and WhatsApp number, and he has not replied within fourteen days. The General Secretary alone declares the player disappeared. This is not a sanction. The player may appeal to the Board of Directors within seven days of learning of the decision. When a player dies, the price of his or her badge, if it has not yet been paid, is paid to the legal heirs, who must prove their relationship, as the Rule Book says.

    8.9A [Locked] Every player may leave the squad and IHCC at any time, of his or her own free will, by giving written notice to the Team Director and the General Secretary. No board, officer or IHCC may force the player to stay, and the player is not treated badly for leaving. The notice period is seven days. The procedure is in the Rule Book.

    8.10 Each Member Board has one representative, whose Gmail address IHCC knows. Until the Team Director of the board is in office, the representative submits the squad on the official IHCC website, as the Rule Book says in Rule Book Chapter 2.

    8.10A [Locked] After the Team Director of a board is in office, new players are registered by the Team Director under Rule Book Chapter 2. The player fills in the joining form on the page of his or her board, signed in with his or her Gmail, with the display name, real name, city, country, WhatsApp number and gender, submits the final form under clause 9.6 and gives the statement under Article 9. The Team Director (or the Deputy Director) makes sure that everything is complete and approves the request within seven days, which sends it to the General Secretary, or refuses it with a written reason. The General Secretary decides within seven days of that approval. The player joins the squad only when the General Secretary approves. The General Secretary also checks that the player is not in another squad, that the cooling-off period under clauses 8.6 and 8.6B is over, that no sanction is in force, and that the squad will not be more than thirty.

    8.11 A member who joins accepts this Constitution in writing, in the final form under Article 9.

    Article 8A: Rights and duties of players

    8A.1 [Locked] Every player has the right:

    (a) To be treated equally, as Article 10 says.

    (b) To be placed in the squad by merit, as clause 8.8 says, and, if in the first twelve, to vote.

    (c) To be heard before any sanction.

    (d) To complain, and to appeal within seven days under clause 25.9.

    (e) To use his or her verified display name while in the squad.

    (f) To be paid the price of the badge under clause 9.8.

    (g) To have personal details kept safe, and, after leaving, to ask for them to be deleted under clause 10.4.

    (h) To receive the official notices of IHCC.

    (i) To leave at any time, under clause 8.9A.

    8A.2 [Locked] Every player has the duty:

    (a) To follow this Constitution, the Rule Book and the decisions of IHCC.

    (b) To give true and correct information, and to keep it correct.

    (c) To be in the squad of one board only, and to use one account only.

    (d) To respect others, and not to abuse or insult anyone.

    (e) Not to fix a match and not to bet.

    (f) To pay a fine decided against him or her within thirty days.

    (g) To use the badge only as IHCC says, and to return it when he or she leaves.

    (h) To keep the Hand Cricket premium of his or her Gmail address active.

    Article 9: Verified names and badges

    9.1 [Locked] A verified display name that a player holds in the Hand Cricket application before submitting the final form under clause 9.6 is the property of that player. No Member Board, officer or IHCC may take it from the player by force or require the player to give it to another.

    9.2 [Locked] A Member Board that wants a verified name held by a player may ask the player for it. The player may give it if he wishes, and is not bound to. This applies whether the name is held by a player of the same board, of another Member Board, or of no Member Board.

    9.3 [Locked] A player who cannot have a name because another person holds it must verify a different name, for example by adding a number or something similar to it.

    9.4 [Locked] After a squad list is final and its verification is complete, every player of the squad must sell to IHCC the verification badge of his or her final display name. This is a condition of taking part in IHCC. A player who does not wish to sell may not take part in IHCC, and keeps his or her badge.

    9.4A [Locked] A new player becomes part of a squad only after he or she has given the statement to IHCC, submitted the final form under clause 9.6 and sold the badge to IHCC under clause 9.4. Without this the player cannot join.

    9.5 [Locked] The price of a badge is 300 Pakistani rupees for a player of Pakistan and 100 Indian rupees for a player of India. These amounts are fixed. A player of any other country is paid the value of 100 Indian rupees. IHCC bears all costs of sending the payment.

    9.6 [Locked] Every player fills in a final form, signing in with his or her own email address. In it the player agrees to this Constitution, to the sale of the badge and to the fines decided under Article 25. IHCC keeps the form as a record. The badge becomes the property of IHCC at the moment the player submits this form, and its price becomes a debt of IHCC to the player. The approval of a parent or guardian is not needed.

    9.7 [Locked] The player keeps the badge on his or her own account for use in IHCC. It is given by IHCC for use only, and does not belong to the player.

    9.8 [Locked] IHCC pays the prices as it receives sponsorship money under Article 23. As money comes in, it is first used to pay the prices owed to persons who have left or been removed, and whose time under clause 9.9 or 9.10 has passed, in the order in which they left. The rest is paid gradually, giving every Member Board an equal turn. For example, if money for twelve badges comes in, one player of each Member Board is paid. Inside a board, the players are paid in the order in which their statements and final forms were accepted, as the Rule Book says. Only the full price is paid, never a part.

    9.9 [Locked] When a player leaves or is removed, the badge is taken off his or her account and kept in reserve on the reserve account of IHCC. It is then given for use to the next player of the same Member Board, as clause 9.13 says. If the price was not yet paid, IHCC must pay it within one week. If IHCC does not have the money to pay it in full within that week, it gets a further week, and a further week each time after that, until the price is paid in full. The price remains a debt of IHCC to the person until it is paid. This applies to one player, to several players and to a whole squad.

    9.10 [Locked] When a whole Member Board leaves, all its badges stay with IHCC, whether or not the prices were paid. Any unpaid prices must be paid within one week, and if IHCC does not have the money, a further week is given in the same way as in clause 9.9. The badges may be given for use to another board.

    9.11 [Locked] A person who has left or been removed must not keep or use the badge. If he does not return it, he may not play in any match of IHCC, IHCC may ask the official developer of the Hand Cricket application and the person's board, and the boards of IHCC may refuse to take part with him. This is also an offence under Rule Book Chapter 6.

    9.12 The purpose of this Article is that verified names always remain with IHCC, so that they can be given to new players of the same Member Board.

    9.13 [Locked] The reserve account is an official account of IHCC, not of any person. It is held by the General Secretary together with the CEO. A new player may take a name that is in reserve for the country of his or her board, and no price is paid for it. Or the player may bring his or her own verified name and sell it to IHCC under clause 9.4, and its price is paid under clause 9.8. When that player leaves, the badge goes back into the reserve. The General Secretary keeps a record of the names in reserve and of the players to whom they were given.

    Article 10: Protection of members

    10.1 No member is treated worse because of nationality, race, colour, religion, gender, language, disability or social standing.

    10.2 IHCC protects younger members. A member under eighteen years of age is treated with extra care. No officer may pressure, mislead or harm him, and any complaint about the safety of a younger member is looked into at once by the Core Committee. An officer speaks to a younger member only about the work of IHCC, in a group or with the Team Director of the board informed. A complaint about the safety of a younger member goes to the General Secretary or the Women's Director, who tell the Core Committee the same day.

    10.3 Every meeting, notice, form and page of IHCC must be usable by blind members with a screen reader.

    10.4 IHCC collects personal details of members, such as real name, Gmail address, WhatsApp number, city and country, only for the work of IHCC. Only the officers who need them for their task may see them. Gmail addresses and WhatsApp numbers are never published. They are shared outside IHCC only with the member's permission, or where the law requires. [Locked] The Gmail address, WhatsApp number and city of a person who has left are deleted on his or her request, except where they are needed for a badge, a debt, a sanction or a complaint. The details are in the Rule Book.

    Chapter III: Governance

    Article 11: Offices inside a Member Board

    11.1 Each Member Board has a Team Director, a Deputy Director and a Media Manager.

    11.2 The Team Director and the Deputy Director are among the twelve Voting Members of the board.

    11.3 [Locked] Each board elects, inside itself, only its Team Director and its Deputy Director. A candidate must be one of the first twelve players of the squad, and only those twelve Voting Members vote. The method of the election, and what to do if votes are equal, is for each board to decide for itself, but the election must be fair.

    11.4 [Locked] The Media Manager is appointed by the elected Team Director and Deputy Director together, of their own choice. The Media Manager may be any of the twelve Voting Members or any other player of the squad. Both must agree. There is no other way of appointing a Media Manager.

    Chapter IV: Elections

    Article 16: Elections

    16.1 Terms. Every office is held for one year. A person may hold the same office for two consecutive terms, but not a third. After two terms he may hold another office, or return to the same office after a gap of one year.

    16.2 Order of the first election. First each board elects its Team Director and Deputy Director and appoints its Media Manager under Article 11. Then the Board of Directors forms the Election Committee under Article 17. Then the Voting Members elect the Chairman, Deputy Chairman and General Secretary. Then the Board of Directors elects the CEO, CFO and Women's Director.

    16.3 The annual election is held before the end of the term. The Election Committee fixes the date, but the election must be finished before the term ends, and no fixed month is needed. The Election Committee is formed at least sixty days before the end of the term. On 3 December the elected persons take charge automatically.

    16.4 Voters and candidates. The Voting Members elect the Chairman, Deputy Chairman and General Secretary. Only a Voting Member can be a candidate for these offices. A candidate stands for one office only.

    16.5 [Locked] Nomination. One Member Board may give only one nominee in all, for only one of the three offices. The proposer of a nominee must be another player of his own board. The seconder must be one person from a different board. When both are in place, the nominee counts. The Election Committee takes all the nominees and conducts the whole election. If there is only one nominee for an office, clause 16.21 applies.

    16.6 [Locked] Campaign. A campaign is allowed, but only up to the day before the day of the election.

    16.7 Campaign conduct. In a campaign, offering money or gifts, pressure, threats and lies are not allowed. They are offences under Rule Book Chapter 6. Where and how a campaign may be made is in Rule Book Chapter 9.

    16.8 [Locked] Result. For each office separately, the candidate who gets the most votes is elected as Chairman, Deputy Chairman or General Secretary for one year.

    16.9 [Locked] No nominee. If no nominee comes forward for an office, the Election Committee extends the date by seven days and announces it again. If still none comes, the Board of Directors elects one of the Voting Members by more than half of all Directors. If the office is still empty after that, the rest of the Core Committee carries on the work.

    16.10 The Voting List. Before the election the Election Committee prepares a list of all Voting Members with their boards and Gmail addresses. It closes the list on a date it fixes before the nominations begin. Squad changes after that are governed by clause 8.7.

    16.11 Voting. Voting is secret. It is held on the official voting page of IHCC, with sign-in to a Google account. A vote is counted only if the Gmail address matches the Voting List. Each voter has one vote for each office. Who voted for whom is never published.

    16.12 Day and time. Voting is held on one day only. The hours are fixed by the Election Committee under Rule Book Chapter 9. The time is not extended.

    16.13 Turnout. An election is valid only if more than half of the Voting Members of the Member Boards vote. In the first election 156 Voting Members are on the Voting List, and at least 79 must vote. In general, at least one more than half of the Voting Members on the Voting List must vote. Every Voting Member on the Voting List may vote, and every vote cast is counted. If not, voting is held again on the same day of the following week, and the votes cast on that day are final, whatever their number. This clause does not apply to a by-election under clause 16.20.

    16.14 Two stages. When voting closes:

    (a) Stage one: on the same day the voting closes, the Election Committee publishes the result with the total votes of each candidate. It also publishes the turnout and the names of those who voted, never how each person voted. The Head of the Election Committee announces the result, together with the confirmation of the other two members, online.

    (b) Stage two: for twenty-four hours after the announcement any member may say that a vote under his name was not cast by him. The Election Committee checks this with the member on his own number. A false vote is cancelled and the member may vote again. If the cancellation changes the result, the Election Committee corrects it and announces the corrected result at once.

    (c) After the twenty-four hours, no objection is accepted, except on clear proof of a false vote, which the Board of Directors decides. In that case the vote is cancelled without a new vote.

    16.15 Equal votes. If votes are equal, the voting is held again between the tied candidates within twenty-four hours. If they are equal again, the Election Committee decides by a toss of a coin in front of everyone.

    16.16 Complaints. Complaints about an election are decided by the Board of Directors within three days, and Article 20 applies to every Director who has an interest. If false votes are found, they are cancelled. If they are so many that they could change the result, the voting for that office is held again. A person who gives or causes a false vote is dealt with under Rule Book Chapter 7.

    16.17 Board participation. If fewer than seven of the twelve Voting Members of a Member Board vote in the election under clause 16.4, the Director of that board may not vote in the election under clause 16.18. There is no other consequence, and no fine. The votes that board's members did cast are counted.

    16.18 CEO, CFO and Women's Director. Every Member Board may give only one nominee in all, for only one of these three offices, on the nominations page of the official IHCC website. The Directors vote on a nominee in a meeting on Google Meet, which is recorded. The Directors' voting is open (clause 19.7A). The result, and how each Director voted, are published. A nominee who gets more than half of all Directors is elected. If no nominee gets so many votes, a second round is held between the two with the most votes. When a Team Director has become a Core officer under clause 14.5, his vote in this election is cast by the new Director of his board if the board reported the new Director to IHCC at least twenty-four hours before the vote, and otherwise by the Deputy Director who acts as Acting Director. If it is the Deputy Director or the Media Manager of a board who has become a Core officer, the Team Director of the board votes as before.

    16.19 The detailed rules of an election, including the nomination procedure on the website, the closing of the Voting List, the hours of voting, and what a voter with a problem must do, are in Rule Book Chapter 9.

    16.20 [Locked] By-election. When an office of Chairman, Deputy Chairman or General Secretary becomes vacant during the term, the Directors form a new Election Committee of three within seven days, as in clause 17.1, and the election is held within thirty days under the rules of this Article, with a new Voting List and the freeze of clause 8.7. If fewer than ninety days of the term remain, there is no by-election, and the acting officer serves until the regular election. The rule of clause 16.13 on more than half of the voters does not apply. The vote must instead be cast by members of at least seven different Member Boards. If not, voting is held again after one week, and then the votes cast are final without this condition. The new officer serves for the remaining term only, and if that is less than six months it does not count as a term under clause 16.1.

    16.20A By-election for the CEO, the CFO or the Women's Director. When one of these offices becomes vacant during the term, the General Secretary opens nominations within seven days. Each Member Board may give one nominee for the vacant office, as in clause 16.18. The nominations stay open for forty-eight hours. Then the Directors vote in a recorded meeting on Google Meet, in open voting, as in clause 16.18, within thirty days of the vacancy. A nominee who gets more than half of all Directors is elected, for the remaining term only. Until then the Chairman appoints a Core member to act (clause 14.16). If fewer than ninety days of the term remain, there is no new vote, and the acting officer serves until the regular election.

    16.21 [Locked] Withdrawal and loss of a nominee. A nominee may withdraw in writing to the Election Committee at least twenty-four hours before voting begins. After that he cannot withdraw, and votes cast for him count. If after a withdrawal, a death, or a disqualification fewer than two nominees remain for an office, the nominations for that office open again for forty-eight hours, for the boards that have not used their nomination or whose nominee has gone. If still only one nominee remains, the voters vote "yes" or "no" on him, and he is elected only if more than half of the votes are "yes". Otherwise clause 16.9 applies. If a nominee is disqualified, the Election Committee gives the reason in writing, and he may object to the Board of Directors within forty-eight hours.

    16.22 Allegation of rigging. The logs of the election stay sealed. They are opened only after an allegation of rigging, by more than half of the Board of Directors (seven of twelve).

    (1) The allegation is made in writing, with evidence, within forty-eight hours of the result.

    (2) It is screened within twenty-four hours, only to see whether there is evidence. If it is rejected, the complainant may go to the Directors within forty-eight hours, and seven of twelve decide.

    (3) The Directors open only the part of the log linked to the allegation. A member of the Election Committee who is the subject of the allegation does not vote.

    (4) The Directors rule that the allegation is true, false, or made in good faith but not proven.

    (5) The opened material is not published, only the ruling and the action.

    Penalty. Only a knowingly false allegation, or one with no basis at all, is punished, at the medium level of Rule Book Chapter 7. A repeat moves to the large level. An allegation made in good faith but not proven has no penalty.

    Who screens. In the first election (15 November 2026) the Election Committee screens the allegation. In later elections the sitting Chairman and General Secretary screen it, and stay in office until the hand-over. If either is a candidate, or the allegation is against him or his board, the Deputy Chairman takes his place. If the Deputy Chairman also cannot sit, the Directors choose two Directors by lot, from boards that have no candidate in that election.

    A candidate has no right to see who voted for him.

    16.23 If the result of a seat is not ready on 3 December, the person who sits in the seat stays until the next person takes charge. A seat whose result is final is handed over on 3 December. Only the disputed seat stays with the outgoing person, and he does routine work only. The Directors may meet at any time and decide what to do. There is no fixed number of days for the dispute.

    Article 20: Conflict of interest

    20.1 A person who has a personal interest, or whose Member Board has an interest, in a particular decision must declare it and must not vote on it. He may speak. This applies to a gain and to a loss.

    20.4 Any member may complain to the Board of Directors if a person did not declare an interest.

    20.5 No officer may accept a gift or money from a sponsor, a board, a candidate or a player that could influence his decision. A small ordinary gift is told to the General Secretary.

    Article 22: Finance

    22.1 IHCC is not registered. Its money is kept in the account of the CFO. A separate account is not required. The CFO gives a written statement that the money is IHCC's and not his own, and keeps a separate written register of every amount. Every spending or transfer needs the earlier written approval of the Chairman (in his absence, the Deputy Chairman). The Chairman receives the monthly bank statement, the General Secretary may see it, and the Auditor compares the statement with the register at least twice a year. At hand-over the new CFO takes the money into his own account in front of the General Secretary, under the written statement of clause 32.13. If the CFO is absent, the Chairman and the General Secretary choose an Acting CFO within two days. If IHCC is registered, the account is opened in its name.

    22.2 Monthly operational fee. Each Member Board pays an equal monthly fee towards the real operating costs of IHCC. These are: the honorarium of the Core Committee, paid officials, commentators, broadcasting, premium or verification badges for deserving members, and other genuine costs of IHCC. Prizes and awards are not paid from the fee. IHCC takes the fee only from Member Boards and never from players. How a board collects it from its players is the board's own affair, and IHCC does not ask for the board's accounts.

    22.3 The fee of a month is the real total cost of that month divided by the number of Member Boards of that month. A new board pays from the month after it joins. A board that gives notice of leaving pays until the date of the notice. The fee of each board is rounded up as the Rule Book says. The amount above the cost becomes credit under clause 22.4. Boards of other countries pay the equal value. The fee is due on the first day of each month, and is payable by the seventh day.

    22.3A If a board cannot pay a month's fee and gives a written reason, the Directors may, by more than half, give it one month's delay, once. Only then do the months of clause 22.7 begin to count. A fee is never waived. If a board removes a player from its squad because the player did not pay, it must follow clause 8.8, tell the General Secretary within twenty-four hours with the reason, and the player may complain to the General Secretary that the procedure was wrong.

    22.4 Any amount left after the costs is held in the operations account as the trust of the boards. It is counted against the next months. When a board's credit covers the cost of a month, no fee is taken for that month.

    22.5 Monthly report. On the first day of each month the CFO publishes a report in the Main Group, with the costs of the last month, the fees received, the credit left, the sponsorship money received and used, and the charity paid. The report also shows how many badge prices are still owed in each board. The queue of the prices owed is public and shows only the display name and the queue number.

    22.6 Auditor. Every year the Board of Directors appoints an Auditor who is not the CFO or a Core officer. He checks the accounts at least twice a year, before the General Assembly meetings, and reports to the Board of Directors and to the General Assembly. If he finds a fault, the Board of Directors acts at once. [Locked] The Auditor checks the accounts of the whole year in the last thirty days of the financial year, and makes a shorter check during the year.

    22.7 Unpaid fees. A board that does not pay for two consecutive months has its rights suspended, including the vote of its Director and the votes of its members in elections. A board that does not pay for four consecutive months may be removed by the Board of Directors by more than two-thirds of all Directors, after a chance to explain.

    22.8 Late payment fine. A fine under Rule Book Chapter 7 may be charged on a late payment. It is not income of IHCC. It is given in charity on behalf of the person who paid it, and IHCC publishes where it was given. The CFO gives it in charity within thirty days of receiving it (clause 25.7).

    22.9 Pay. The pay of the Core Committee is fixed by the Board of Directors, published, and may be changed by more than half of all Directors.

    22.10 [Locked] The financial year of IHCC runs from 3 December to 2 December, with the term of the Core Committee. The first financial year runs from 3 December 2026 to 2 December 2027.

    22.11 [Replaced by clause 22.1.] The accounts of IHCC are kept and operated as clause 22.1 says.

    22.12 [Locked] The CFO who ran the year prepares the report of the whole year and signs it, before 2 December or at the hand-over. The new CFO publishes it, with the report of the Auditor, in the Main Group within thirty days of the end of the financial year.

    22.13 [Locked] When an officer hands over, he gives the amount of money, the debts, the queue of badge prices and the list of unpaid fees to the new officer in writing, and the General Secretary records it.

    22.14 Spending is approved in three levels: small, medium and large. The Rule Book fixes the amounts.

    (a) Small: the CFO and the Chairman in writing.

    (b) Medium: more than half of the Core Committee in writing.

    (c) Large, or above the large limit in a year for one purpose: the Board of Directors by more than half.

    The payment of a badge price under clause 9.8 is a fixed debt and needs only the CFO's record. In an emergency the Chairman may spend alone up to the small limit and reports to the Directors within fourteen days. The Directors approve the yearly budget by purposes (clause 13.2(a)), and the Core Committee may spend inside the budget. Every payment has a receipt or proof and a reason in the CFO's record, and the monthly report shows the total and the purpose. The amounts may be changed by the Directors after advice.

    Article 23: Sponsorship and voluntary contributions

    23.1 Prizes and awards of the tournaments and world cups of IHCC are paid for by sponsors, not by the fees of Member Boards. The prizes of a series between boards are as clause 27.3C says.

    23.2 Any person may give or bring a sponsor of his own free will, including a Core officer, a Director, a member, or a person outside IHCC.

    23.3 A sponsorship up to the limit that the Rule Book fixes is approved by the Chairman. If the sponsor is the Chairman himself or his relative, the Deputy Chairman approves it. A sponsorship above that limit is approved by the Board of Directors by more than half of all Directors. The limit applies to one sponsorship, and not to the total of a year. The sponsor of a series between boards is approved by the General Secretary under clause 27.3C. A sponsor is not accepted if his business is gambling, interest or alcohol.

    23.4 A sponsor, or a member who gives money, gets no extra vote, office, or favor, and is treated equally in discipline. A sponsor does not interfere in the decisions of IHCC.

    23.5 Sponsorship money is used only for the purpose for which it was given. It goes only into the account of the CFO. Anyone else who receives it hands it to the CFO within three days and tells the General Secretary.

    23.6 IHCC publicly thanks sponsors and encourages members to give.

    23.7 A tournament offers a cash prize only when a sponsor has promised the prize. If there is none, it is announced clearly that there is no cash prize.

    23.7A The prizes of an IHCC tournament may include the winner, the runner-up, the third place, the player of the match, the best batter, the best bowler and other awards. All of them are paid from sponsorship money, and IHCC gives them step by step when it sees fit.

    23.8 Sponsorship money may be used to pay the prices of badges owed under Article 9. Money given for another purpose is used only for that purpose.

    23.9 The General Secretary keeps a written agreement for each sponsorship. A sponsor gets only a public acknowledgment of the name. If the purpose of a sponsorship is cancelled, IHCC asks the sponsor and holds the money for sixty days. A public record is kept every month.

    23.10 General sponsorship money without any condition may be used anywhere under clause 22.14. Money given for a particular series or purpose is used only for that.

    Chapter VII: Discipline and disputes

    Article 25: Discipline

    25.1 [Locked] Who may complain. Any member (player), officer or Member Board may make a complaint, in writing to the General Secretary or in the Main Group. A person outside IHCC may only give information to the General Secretary, who may start a complaint himself if there is proof. A complaint without a name is not accepted. A person who fears may give his name to the General Secretary and ask that it is not shown to the accused, but the accused is always told what is said against him, so that he can answer.

    25.1A [Locked] Time limit for a complaint. A complaint is made within ninety days of the act or of the day it became known. For serious offences (match fixing, a false vote or buying votes, misuse of IHCC money, threats and pressure) the time is one year. If the act was hidden, the time runs from the day it was found out.

    25.2 [Locked] Procedure and time. The General Secretary acknowledges the complaint in writing within twenty-four hours and tells the accused. The accused has three days to answer. For every complaint the Core Committee has one investigating officer, a Core member whose board has nothing to do with the complaint and who has no relationship or dispute with the accused. This is first the General Secretary (unless he is concerned), then the Deputy Chairman, then a Core member chosen by the Chairman in writing. The investigating officer takes written statements from both sides, looks at the proof, and gives a written report within fourteen days (once seven more, with a written reason). He does not vote on the matter. The other Core members decide within seven days of the report (more than half; a member whose board is concerned, and the accused, do not vote). If fewer than three Core members can vote, the Board of Directors decides it at once as the first instance (a Director whose board is concerned does not vote), and the appeal is to the full Board under clause 25.9C. The investigating officer may ask any player or Voting Member for a written answer, and the General Secretary keeps the record. The whole matter takes at most thirty days. If this time passes, the Chairman gives the reason in the Main Group and gives seven days more. Appeals are as in clause 25.9.

    25.2A [Locked] Complaints against a Core officer. If the complaint is against the General Secretary, the Deputy Chairman takes it and runs the matter. If it is against the Chairman, the Deputy Chairman acts for him (clause 14.11). If it is against any other Core officer, the General Secretary takes it and the Chairman hears it. The accused and any member whose Member Board is concerned do not vote. If fewer than four Core officers remain, or the complaint is against two or more Core officers, the Board of Directors decides it at once as the first instance (a Director whose board is concerned does not vote), and the appeal is the full Board again under clause 25.9C.

    25.2B [Locked] Interim measure. For a serious offence only (match fixing, a false vote or buying votes, threats and pressure, misuse of IHCC money, revenge on a person who complained), and only if the complaint has written proof, the Chairman and the General Secretary together may in writing, with the reason, stop the accused for the time of the inquiry from voting, from the work of his office and from contact with the complainant and the witnesses. He is not removed from the squad and his badge stays. The Board of Directors must confirm it by more than half within seven days, or it ends by itself. The accused may object in writing within forty-eight hours. It is not a sanction. If he is found innocent, the record says so and he gets back what he lost. A false complaint is an offence, and an officer who misuses this clause is responsible. It lasts at most thirty days.

    25.3 [Locked] Three levels. The Core Committee decides the sanction in the first instance, by more than half of the Core members who can vote on the matter. A member who has an interest does not vote, and a member who can vote but does not is not counted as a yes. Removal of a player from a squad is decided first by the Core Committee, and may be appealed to the full Board of Directors. The sanctions are: a warning, a suspension, removal from a squad, a ban from tournaments and series, removal from an office, and a fine under Rule Book Chapter 7. The Rule Book fixes which sanction is small, medium or large. A repeated offence, the third time, moves to the next level. Some offences are always large, as the Rule Book says.

    25.3A [Locked] Matters decided by the Board of Directors at first instance. The suspension or removal of a Member Board (Article 7) and the removal of a Core officer (clauses 13.5 and 13.5A) are decided by the Board of Directors, and not by the Core Committee.

    25.3B [Locked] Bans. A usual ban from tournaments, series or squads lasts from six months to two years. A permanent ban may be imposed only for the gravest offences (match fixing, a false vote or buying votes, and misuse of IHCC money), and only by the Board of Directors, by more than two-thirds of the Directors who can vote on the matter (with twelve Directors, nine).

    25.4 A person facing the complaint does not vote on it and has the right to explain.

    25.5 [Locked] No sanction without a rule. A person is sanctioned only for an act that was an offence in this Constitution or the Rule Book at the time he did it. A later rule does not apply to what was done before. For an act that is not written as an offence, only a small sanction is possible (a warning, a reprimand or a written apology), and the Core Committee decides it. If the Board of Directors thinks that an act should be an offence, it writes it into the Rule Book by more than half of all Directors, with a notice of at least seven days, and only for the future. One act is sanctioned once. If a Member Board and IHCC both sanction one act, the sanction of the board counts in the sanction of IHCC.

    25.6 [Locked] The kinds of sanctions include a ban from matches, a suspension, and fines of money. What each offence leads to is decided in Rule Book Chapter 7. [Locked] Every fine is fixed in Pakistani rupees. A player of another country pays the equal value.

    25.7 [Locked] A fine is given in charity on behalf of the person fined, to another institution or welfare organisation. IHCC must not use the money of any fine for its own purposes, including the repayment of its debts, the prices of badges and its working expenses. A fine and a debt of IHCC are separate and are never set off against each other. IHCC keeps a public record of the fines received, from whom, and where each was given. The CFO gives each fine in charity within thirty days of receiving it, keeps it apart from other money, and the Chairman and the CFO together choose the charity. [Locked] When a player leaves, a fine that he or she has not paid does not end. The player cannot join any squad until it is paid.

    25.8 [Locked] The main purpose of every sanction, with money or without, is to keep good manners and a well-organised system. It is not to earn money.

    25.9 [Locked] Appeal. A small sanction cannot be appealed, and the decision of the Core Committee is final. A medium sanction can be appealed to a committee of five Directors chosen by lot. A large sanction can be appealed to the full Board of Directors. The lot is drawn by the General Secretary in front of everyone. The committee has no Director whose board is the board of the accused or of the complainant, or who took part in the matter. A Director who has an interest does not vote.

    25.9A [Locked] Grounds. An appeal is heard only on one of these grounds: (a) new proof that was not there before; (b) a fault of procedure, for example that the accused had no chance to answer or that a member with an interest voted; (c) the sanction is far too heavy for the offence; (d) the Core Committee went beyond its powers. The appeal is in writing, with the reason and proof, to the General Secretary within seven days. The General Secretary checks within twenty-four hours whether there is a ground. If not, he refuses it with a written reason, and the person may object once in writing within forty-eight hours. The committee decides within fourteen days. Its decision is final. It may uphold the sanction, reduce it or cancel it, but it cannot make it heavier. A committee of five decides by three votes. The full Board decides by more than half, but to change a large sanction it needs more than two-thirds of all Directors. If the appeal was made by false proof or deceit, a separate complaint can be made.

    25.9B [Locked] An appeal does not stop the sanction. It takes effect from the day of the decision. Only the thirty days for paying a fine are paused during the appeal, and begin again seven days after the decision on the appeal, because a fine is given in charity and cannot be taken back. If the appeal succeeds, the sanction is cancelled or reduced, the record is cleared, the time already served counts in the new decision, the office or the place in the squad is given back, and the Main Group is told that the sanction was wrong. During the appeal the duties of the sanction stay, and breaking them is a separate offence.

    25.9C [Locked] A decision of the Board of Directors at first instance (clause 25.2, 25.2A or 25.3A) can be appealed once, on the grounds of clause 25.9A, to the full Board again, and to change it more than two-thirds of all Directors are needed.

    25.10 A suspension lasts up to six months. It may be extended once for up to six months by more than half. During it the person may not vote or hold office, but he may complain and appeal.

    25.11 A suspension ends when its time ends or when its condition is met, such as the payment of arrears.

    25.12 The removal of an officer is made by the Board of Directors under clauses 13.5 and 13.5A. The officer concerned does not vote. He is given a written reason and a chance to explain.

    25.13 [Locked] Every player agrees in the final form under clause 9.6 that he or she will pay the fines decided under this Constitution.

    25.14 [Locked] A player removed from a squad as a sanction may not join any squad for the length of the ban, and his or her badge stays with IHCC. A complaint continues even if the person leaves while it is pending, and the decision applies if he or she returns.

    25.15 [Locked] Notice and record. A small sanction is told only to the person and the Core Committee, not in the Main Group (the public record of fines in clause 25.7 stays). For a medium or large sanction the Main Group is told only the display name, the kind of offence, the sanction and its length, and not the real name, Gmail, WhatsApp or details. The notice is taken out of the Main Group when the sanction ends. IHCC keeps the record of a small sanction for two years, of a medium sanction for five years, and of a large sanction always. The old record is used only for clause 25.3 (repeat offences) and for the permission to return, and the person is clean after the sanction.

    25.16 [Locked] Pardon. Only the Board of Directors may forgive or reduce a sanction, and no single officer. At least half of the sanction must have passed without a new offence. A medium sanction needs more than half of all Directors, and a large sanction more than two-thirds. The person asks in writing through the General Secretary, with a reason, and the decision is taken within fourteen days. A small sanction is not forgiven. A person under a permanent ban may ask to be forgiven after two years, and he is let back only by more than two-thirds of the Directors who can vote on the matter (with twelve Directors, nine). If the request is refused, he may ask again after two more years. A general pardon for everyone is not allowed. The record stays, and clause 25.3 on repeated offences still applies.

    Chapter VIII: Operations

    Article 27: Tournaments and series

    27.1 The Core Committee proposes a tournament of IHCC, and the Board of Directors approves it by more than half. A series between Member Boards is approved under clause 27.3A.

    27.2 For the tournaments of IHCC, the costs of the Core Committee's honorarium, officials, commentators, broadcasting and live streaming are paid from the operational fee, which is divided equally among the Member Boards. For a series between Member Boards, clauses 27.3 to 27.3D apply.

    27.3 A series may be played between two Member Boards (a bilateral series), or between three or four Member Boards (a multi-board series). In every case it is a series, in which each board plays more than one match. The boards decide everything among themselves first: the number of matches, the number of T20, ODI and Test matches, the dates, the officials (volunteer or paid), the sponsors, the budget, and who pays what. They send all of it in writing to the General Secretary.

    27.3A The General Secretary alone approves the series. The approval of the Chairman is not needed. He also checks for a clash with an event of IHCC, and if there is a clash he may refuse the series or ask the boards to move it. The series may be played only after the approval. Within twenty-four hours of the approval he announces the series in the Main Group (the boards, the number of matches and the dates) and tells the CEO, who follows it. Every three months he gives the Board of Directors a report on the series played.

    27.3B IHCC gives the officials of the series. Officials may be volunteers who take no fee. If they are paid, the boards of the series bear the whole cost, and IHCC pays nothing for a series.

    27.3C The prizes of a series (for example player of the match, the winner of the series, and second and third places) are decided by the boards. They say in their request what the budget is and who gives it. A board may give a prize from its own money. A sponsor of a series may be found by the boards, and the General Secretary approves the sponsor together with the series; the approval of the Directors under clause 23.3 is not needed for it. A sponsor whose business is gambling, interest or alcohol is still not accepted. The sponsor's money is paid into the account of the CFO (clause 23.5), and IHCC pays it out for the prizes of that series.

    27.3D The boards give IHCC the scorecards (CSV) and the results of every match of the series, so that IHCC keeps the record. The results of every approved series count in the rankings of the boards.

    27.4 IHCC keeps the official records and results of its events and of the approved series, and may publish rankings of the boards. The method of the rankings is in the Rule Book and is approved by the Board of Directors.

    Chapter IX: General provisions

    Article 29: Indemnity and confidentiality

    29.1 No Director, officer or member who acts honestly and in good faith for IHCC is to be blamed, fined or removed for a mistake made while doing so. This does not protect dishonesty, a deliberate wrong, or a breach of this Constitution.

    29.2 What is said in a closed meeting of the Board of Directors or the Core Committee stays confidential until the decision is announced in the Main Group. This does not apply to the open voting of Directors under clause 19.7A.

    29.3 Breaking this confidentiality is an offence under the Rule Book.

    Article 30: Amendment

    30.1 A proposal to amend an Article is made in writing by at least two Directors or by the Core Committee, and deals with one topic only. The full text is sent to the Main Group, by email and on WhatsApp, at least seven days before the vote. The vote is in a recorded meeting. More than two-thirds of all Directors (nine of twelve) are needed, and each Director's vote is published. The amendment takes effect seven days after the vote. The General Secretary corrects the references in the Rule Book within fourteen days.

    30.2 The Rule Book may be amended by more than half of all Directors, under Rule Book rule 1.4.

    30.3 No single person or officer may amend this Constitution or the Rule Book.

    30.4 The Rule Book cannot amend this Constitution.

    Article 31: Dissolution

    31.1 IHCC may be dissolved only if the Board of Directors decides by more than two-thirds of all Directors, and then the Voting Members decide by more than two-thirds of all Voting Members.

    31.2 On dissolution all badges stay in the account of IHCC. Players who were already paid keep the price paid. Players who are not paid are paid from the money that remains, in queue order. If the money runs out, the badge of an unpaid player goes back to him or her and the debt ends.

    31.3 The website, groups, logo, email and records go as the last decision of the Directors says, or stay in IHCC's accounts.

    31.4 The accounts are settled within ninety days. Debts are paid first, then the credit of the boards is returned in equal shares, and unused sponsorship money is returned to the sponsors or given in charity as the Directors decide.
