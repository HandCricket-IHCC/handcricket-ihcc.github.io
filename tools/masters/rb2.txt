THE IHCC RULE BOOK
Draft 5 (14 November 2026), Part 2 of 2: Chapters 8 to 13. Part 1 holds the header, the contents and Chapters 1 to 7. The notes at the head of Part 1 apply to the whole Rule Book.

CHAPTER 8: COMPLAINTS, APPEALS AND LEAVING

Part A: Complaints and appeals
8.1 [Locked] A complaint is made in writing to the General Secretary, by email to the official IHCC address or in the Main Group. It says who, what and when, and gives the proof. It is made within ninety days of the act or of the day it became known, and within one year for serious offences (match fixing, a false vote or buying a vote, misuse of IHCC money, threats and pressure). If the act was hidden, the time runs from the day it was found out. A complaint without a name is not accepted. A person who fears may give his name to the General Secretary and ask that it is not shown to the accused, but the accused is told everything said against him so that he can answer. A person outside IHCC only gives information, and the General Secretary may start a complaint himself if there is proof.
8.2 [Locked] The two sides may first try to settle the matter among themselves or inside their own board, but this does not stop the times in rule 8.4.
8.3 [Locked] The General Secretary acknowledges a complaint in writing within twenty-four hours and tells the accused. The accused has three days to answer. The Core Committee has one investigating officer for each complaint: a Core member whose board has nothing to do with the complaint and who has no relationship or dispute with the accused. This is first the General Secretary (unless he is concerned), then the Deputy Chairman, then a Core member chosen in writing by the Chairman (Constitution clause 25.2).
8.4 [Locked] The investigating officer takes written statements from both sides, looks at the proof, and gives a written report within fourteen days (once seven more, with a written reason). He does not vote on the matter. The other Core members decide within seven days of the report, by more than half of the Core members who can vote on the matter. The accused and any member whose board is concerned do not vote, and a member who can vote but does not is not counted as a yes. If fewer than three Core members can vote, the Board of Directors decides it at once as the first instance, and the appeal is to the full Board (Constitution clauses 25.2 and 25.9C, rule 8.35). The decision is in writing with the reason. The whole matter takes at most thirty days. If this time passes, the Chairman gives the reason in the Main Group and gives seven days more. The investigating officer may ask any player or Voting Member for a written answer, and the General Secretary keeps the record.
8.5 [Locked] An appeal is made within seven days, as Constitution clauses 25.9 to 25.9C say, and rules 8.30 to 8.34.
8.6 Complaints about an election are decided as Constitution clause 16.16 says.
8.6A A member may ask the Core Committee in writing, within seven days, to review a decision of the General Secretary. The General Secretary does not vote on the review, and the decision of the Core Committee is final (Constitution clause 14.12A).
8.7 Every complaint and decision is kept in a record, which only the persons concerned and the Board of Directors may see.

Part B: A player leaves
8.8 [Locked] A player may leave the squad and IHCC at any time. He gives written notice from his registered Gmail address to the Team Director and the General Secretary. No reason is needed.
8.9 [Locked] The notice period is seven days. The board cannot stop him. The Team Director may only politely ask him to think again, and this is not pressure.
8.10 [Locked] At the end of the notice the player leaves the squad. His badge goes to the reserve account (rule 2.15) and the next player moves up under Constitution clause 8.9. The General Secretary changes the record within twenty-four hours.
8.11 [Locked] If the player is a Voting Member and the Voting List is closed, Constitution clause 8.7 applies: he keeps his name on the list, may vote, and is replaced after the election.
8.12 [Locked] A person who leaves is asked for his decision politely and not under pressure. Telling lies about IHCC after leaving is not allowed under rule 6.8.
8.12A A person who wants to leave an office gives written notice by email at least seven days before. No reason is needed.

Part C: A player who is removed or banned
8.13 [Locked] A player who is removed under Constitution Article 25 is banned for a time of not less than six months and not more than two years. The Core Committee decides first, and the appeal is to the full Board of Directors (Constitution clause 25.3). A permanent ban is possible only under Constitution clause 25.3B. His badge goes to the reserve account. If the price was not paid, it is paid within one week (Constitution clause 9.9).
8.14 [Locked] The fine and the badge price are never set off against each other. The player may return only with the approval of the General Secretary, after the ban and after the cooling-off of ninety days. His old display name is closed during the ban. If he returns under another person's Gmail address or name, that is false information and wrong use of a verified name, and his ban is doubled.
8.15 [Locked] The General Secretary announces only the display name and the length of the ban.

Part D: A player who disappears
8.16 [Locked] If a player cannot be reached, the Team Director tries by WhatsApp and email for thirty days, and then reports to the General Secretary. The General Secretary sends a written notice to the registered Gmail address and WhatsApp number, with fourteen days to reply. If there is no reply, the General Secretary alone declares the player "disappeared". This is not a sanction. A player whose premium lapses and who does not renew it is treated in the same way (Constitution clause 8.4). The player may appeal to the Board of Directors within seven days. His badge goes to the reserve account. If the price was not paid, it stays a debt of IHCC and is paid when he proves his identity from his registered Gmail address. This right does not expire. If he wants to return, the ninety days under rule 8.17 apply.

Part E: Cooling-off and transfers
8.17 [Locked] A player who has left IHCC may join a squad again, of the same board or another, only after ninety days from the date of leaving.
8.18 [Locked] A player who was removed under Constitution Article 25 may not join any squad until the sanction ends, and then the ninety days apply.
8.19 [Locked] Transfer to another board. A player may transfer once in a year. He gives notice in writing to the old board and the General Secretary. The new board accepts, and may refuse without a reason. The player joins the new squad only after thirty days from the notice and is in no squad meanwhile. No transfer is made from the closing of the Voting List until the result of the election.
8.20 [Locked] Leaving IHCC and returning, and a transfer, are counted together in the one-in-a-year rule (Constitution clause 8.6).
8.21 [Locked] What happens to the badge in a transfer:
    (a) The old badge goes to the reserve account and is given to the next player of the old board. A transfer is not "leaving" for the payment of the price in one week.
    (b) During the thirty days the player verifies a new display name of a real cricketer of the country of the new board, at his own cost, and submits a new final form (rule 2.13).
    (c) The new badge becomes the property of IHCC when the form is submitted. Its price is a new debt, paid in its turn under rule 2.14, at the rates of Constitution clause 9.5 for the country of the new board.
    (d) If the old price was not paid, it stays a separate debt and is paid in its turn, not within one week. So a player may have two debts.
    (e) If the name is held by another person, Constitution clause 9.3 applies.
    (f) If the player does not verify the new name in thirty days, the transfer is cancelled. He may go back to the old board only if a place is free there. Otherwise he is treated as having left, and rule 8.17 applies.
8.22 [Locked] A player who is in no squad during a transfer cannot vote and cannot play for any board.

Part F: Fines and leaving
8.23 [Locked] An unpaid fine does not end when a player leaves. It is due in thirty days. If it is not paid, the person is suspended under Constitution clauses 25.10 and 25.11. He cannot return or transfer until it is paid, and then the ninety days apply.

Part G: Death of a player
8.24 [Locked] The death of a player is reported to the General Secretary with one confirmation, for example by the Team Director or a family member. The player leaves the squad that day and the next player moves up. No ban and no ninety days apply. His badge goes to the reserve account.
8.25 [Locked] If the price was not paid, it is paid to the legal heirs within one week of the day they prove their relationship.
8.26 [Locked] His personal data is deleted within thirty days unless a debt is pending.
8.27 [Locked] If the dead person was a Voting Member on a closed list, he is counted as not having voted, and he is replaced after the election. If he held an office, the vacancy is filled under Constitution clauses 11.8 or 14.16.
8.28 [Locked] The General Secretary may issue a short condolence notice.

Part H: Discipline procedure
8.29 [Locked] Interim measure (Constitution clause 25.2B). It needs written proof, the written approval of the Chairman and the General Secretary together, and a written reason. It stops the accused from voting, from the work of his office and from contact with the complainant and the witnesses, and it does not remove him from the squad. The Board of Directors confirms it by more than half within seven days, or it ends. The accused may object in writing in forty-eight hours. It ends in thirty days at the latest. If he is found innocent, the record says that it was not a sanction.
8.30 [Locked] Appeal. A small sanction has no appeal. For a medium sanction the General Secretary draws five Directors by lot in front of everyone, for example in a Google Meet. The committee has no Director whose board is the board of the accused or of the complainant, or who took part in the matter. For a large sanction the full Board of Directors hears the appeal. A Director who has an interest does not vote.
8.31 [Locked] An appeal is in writing, with the reason and the proof, to the General Secretary within seven days. It must be on one of these grounds: new proof; a fault of procedure; a sanction far too heavy for the offence; or that the Core Committee went beyond its powers. The General Secretary checks within twenty-four hours, and if there is no ground, refuses it with a written reason. The person may object once in writing within forty-eight hours.
8.32 [Locked] The committee decides within fourteen days. It may uphold, reduce or cancel the sanction, and cannot make it heavier. A committee of five decides by three votes. The full Board decides by more than half, and to change a large sanction it needs more than two-thirds of all Directors (nine of twelve). Its decision is final.
8.33 [Locked] An appeal does not stop the sanction. Only the thirty days for paying a fine are paused, and begin again seven days after the decision on the appeal. If the appeal succeeds, the sanction is cancelled or reduced, the record is cleared, the time already served counts, the office or place in the squad is given back, and the Main Group is told that the sanction was wrong. During an appeal the duties of the sanction stay.
8.34 [Locked] A first-instance decision of the Board of Directors (a Member Board, a Core officer, or a complaint against Core officers under rule 8.35) can be appealed once, on the same grounds, to the full Board again, and more than two-thirds of all Directors are needed to change it.
8.35 [Locked] Complaints against Core officers. Against the General Secretary: the Deputy Chairman takes the complaint and runs it. Against the Chairman: the Deputy Chairman acts for him. Against another Core officer: the General Secretary takes it and the Chairman hears it. The accused and members whose board is concerned do not vote. If fewer than four Core officers remain, or the complaint is against two or more of them, the full Board of Directors decides it at once.
8.36 [Locked] Notice and record. A small sanction is told only to the person and the Core Committee. For a medium or large sanction the Main Group gets only the display name, the kind of offence, the sanction and its length. The notice is taken out when the sanction ends. IHCC keeps the record of a small sanction two years, of a medium one five years, and of a large one always. The old record is used only for repeat offences and for the permission to return.
8.37 [Locked] Pardon. Only the Board of Directors may forgive or reduce a sanction, when at least half of it has passed without a new offence. A medium sanction needs more than half of all Directors, and a large sanction more than two-thirds. The person asks in writing through the General Secretary with a reason, and the Directors decide within fourteen days. A small sanction is not forgiven. A person under a permanent ban may ask to be forgiven after two years, and he is let back only by more than two-thirds of the Directors who can vote on the matter (nine of twelve). If the request is refused, he may ask again after two more years. A general pardon for everyone is not allowed. The record stays.
8.38 [Locked] A Member Board suspended for its own fault: its players do not take part in IHCC tournaments and series, and do not vote in elections. Their squad, names and badges stay, and their badge prices stay in the queue. They may move to another board that accepts them without the thirty days and without counting the move. The board registers no new players. When the suspension ends, a player who moved away may return at once if a place is free.

Part I: A Member Board leaves IHCC (Constitution clause 5.4A)
8.39 At least eight of the twelve Voting Members of a board write to the General Secretary, from their registered Gmail addresses, that the board leaves. The Director alone cannot do this.
8.40 The General Secretary checks the names and the Gmail addresses, announces the notice in the Main Group, and within twenty-four hours tells every player of the board (one to thirty) by email and WhatsApp. The notice runs for thirty days.
8.41 A player who does not wish to go with the board tells the General Secretary in writing. He may move to another Member Board that accepts him without the thirty days of rule 8.19 and without counting it in the one move in a year.
8.42 A board cannot leave from the closing of the Voting List until the result of the election, nor during a tournament in progress. A board that has left cannot join again for ninety days, and then only as a new board (rule 8.43).

Part J: A new Member Board joins (Constitution clauses 5.3 and 5.3A)
8.43 A new board sends the General Secretary a written request with its squad list of at least twelve players. For each player it gives the display name, real name, Gmail address and WhatsApp number.
8.44 The General Secretary checks within fourteen days: each display name belongs to a real cricketer of the country of the board and is verified (rule 2.12); every Gmail and WhatsApp number is different; no player is in the squad of another board; there is one board for that country. He reports to the Directors, who decide by more than two-thirds.
8.45 The first six months are a trial. During it the Director's vote is not counted on a decision that needs more than two-thirds (Constitution clause 5.3A). After six months the Directors confirm the board by more than half, or extend the trial once, for three months. A false or invented squad means suspension at once.

Part K: A Core officer who does not answer, and leave (Constitution clause 14.18)
8.46 If a Core officer has not answered official matters for fourteen days and gave no leave notice, the General Secretary (or, if it is the General Secretary, the Deputy Chairman) gives him written notice on email and WhatsApp, with seven days to answer.
8.47 If he does not answer, the Chairman (or the Deputy Chairman) declares the office vacant in writing. The Directors confirm it within seven days by more than half, or it ends. It is not a sanction. The officer may be heard again within seven days. The vacancy is filled under Constitution clauses 14.16 and 16.20.
8.48 Leave: a Core officer may take written leave of up to thirty days, once a year, approved by the Chairman (for the Chairman, by the Deputy Chairman). An acting officer works meanwhile. A Team Director who is absent from three Directors' meetings in a row without a written reason is replaced at the meetings by his Deputy Director, and his board is given seven days' notice to choose a new Director. This is not a sanction.

CHAPTER 9: ELECTION RULES AND TIMETABLE
9.1 The Election Committee makes the timetable of an election and announces it in the Main Group. It may move a date only for a good reason.
9.2 The Election Committee closes the Voting List on a date it fixes before the nominations begin. After that Constitution clause 8.7 applies.
9.3 Nominations for Chairman, Deputy Chairman and General Secretary are made by the Team Director of each Member Board on the nominations page of the official IHCC website. This page is separate from the voting page. The page does not accept a nomination that breaks the rules of Constitution Article 16.
9.4 A campaign is not made on the official IHCC website. It may be made in other places, such as groups and meetings, up to the day before the election.
9.5 Voting is held on one day, for twelve hours, in Pakistan time. In the first election the hours are from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m. The voting page opens and closes by itself at these times, and a vote outside them is not accepted. The time is not extended.
9.6 A voter who cannot sign in or cannot vote tells the Election Committee by a call on Google Meet or WhatsApp. The Committee first tries to solve the problem. If it cannot, the Head of the Election Committee records the call, and then enters the voter's vote himself on the election admin page, writing the time of the call and a note. The Head keeps the recording as proof, sealed like the logs (rule 9.36). Only the Head may enter a vote in this way. A text message alone is not accepted.
9.7 The Head of the Election Committee announces the result online, together with the confirmation of the other two members.
9.8 Nominations for CEO, CFO and Women's Director are made by the Team Directors on the same nominations page. The Election Committee fixes the dates.
9.9 The steps of a nomination follow one another, and a step opens only when the step before it is done. First, the Team Director chooses the nominee, who may be the Director himself, and the office. Second, the nominee confirms with his own Gmail address. Third, the proposer, who may be any other Voting Member of the same board, confirms. Fourth, the seconder, one named Voting Member of another board whom the Director has chosen, confirms; only that person can confirm. Fifth, the Team Director locks the nomination.
9.10 The nominee confirms in his own step even when he is the Director himself.
9.11 A nomination that is not yet locked is incomplete and does not count. Incomplete nominations are seen only by the Election Committee. A locked nomination is shown to all voters, with the name, the board and the office.
9.12 The Team Director may revoke a nomination within twenty-four hours after he has locked it. A revoked nomination is removed completely, with the nominee, the proposer and the seconder. A nomination cannot be edited. After the twenty-four hours it is final.
9.13 Each Member Board has two attempts in all. After a first nomination has been revoked, and while the nominations are still open, the board may nominate once more. The second nomination, once locked, cannot be revoked.
9.14 One person may be the seconder of only one nominee in an election. One person stands for only one office, and one board has only one nominee, as Constitution clause 16.5 says.
9.15 The nominations close by themselves at the end of the last day. In the first election this is 11:59 p.m. on Tuesday 10 November. Only the nominations that are locked at that time stand in the election.
9.16 The page shows each person the confirmations waiting for him. IHCC sends no email for this. The Team Director and the persons concerned tell one another themselves.
9.17 The Election Committee may pause or close the voting early by hand only when the voting service fails or is attacked, as Constitution clause 19.9 describes. Every such action is recorded and announced in the Main Group at once. Whether the time lost is added back: [to be decided].
9.18 Every action of the Election Committee on the election pages is recorded in a log with who did it, when, and what. During an election the log is seen only by the Election Committee. The log stays sealed. It is opened only under rule 9.36.
9.19 to 9.31 [The timetable of the first election is in Chapter 13, the Transitional Schedule, at the end of this Rule Book.]
9.32 The drafting of the Constitution and the Rule Book is frozen on 30 November 2026, which is the last day for changes. A final check is made on 2 December, and both documents come into force on 3 December 2026 (Constitution clause 32.1).
9.33 [Locked] By-election (Constitution clause 16.20). The Directors form a new Election Committee within seven days. The election is held within thirty days, with a new Voting List and the freeze of Constitution clause 8.7. There is no by-election if fewer than ninety days of the term remain. The rule of more than half of the voters does not apply, but the votes must come from at least seven different Member Boards. If not, voting is held again after one week and the votes cast are final.
9.34 [Locked] Withdrawal of a nominee (Constitution clause 16.21). A nominee withdraws in writing to the Election Committee at least twenty-four hours before voting begins. If fewer than two nominees remain for an office, the nominations for it open again for forty-eight hours for the boards that have not used their nomination or whose nominee has gone. If only one nominee remains, the voters vote "yes" or "no", and he is elected only if more than half of the votes are "yes".
9.35 Secret voting (Constitution clause 16.11). Who voted for whom is never published. On the same day the voting closes, the result, the total votes of each candidate, the turnout and the names of those who voted are published (stage one). For the next twenty-four hours any member may say that a vote under his name was not cast by him (stage two, Constitution clause 16.14). A voter receives a receipt of his own vote by email, only for himself.
9.36 Allegation of rigging (Constitution clause 16.22). The logs of the election stay sealed. A written allegation with evidence is made within forty-eight hours of the result. It is screened within twenty-four hours, only to see whether there is evidence. In the first election the Election Committee screens it. In later elections the sitting Chairman and General Secretary screen it; if either is a candidate, or the allegation is against him or his board, the Deputy Chairman takes the place; if the Deputy Chairman also cannot sit, the Directors choose two Directors by lot from boards that have no candidate in that election. If the allegation is rejected, the complainant may go to the Directors within forty-eight hours, and seven of twelve decide. The Directors (seven of twelve) may open only the part of the log linked to the allegation. A member of the Election Committee who is the subject of the allegation does not vote. The Directors rule that the allegation is true, false, or made in good faith but not proven. The opened material is not published, only the ruling and the action. Only a knowingly false allegation, or one with no basis at all, is punished, at the medium level (rule 7.14(b)); a repeat moves to the large level. A good-faith allegation that is not proven has no penalty.
9.37 Term end (Constitution clauses 16.3 and 16.23). The Election Committee fixes the date of the annual election, but the election must be finished before the term ends. The Election Committee is formed at least sixty days before the end of the term. On 3 December the elected persons take charge automatically. A seat whose result is final is handed over on 3 December. If the result of a seat is not ready, the person sitting in it stays until the next person takes charge and does routine work only. The Directors may meet at any time and decide. There is no fixed number of days.
9.38 Directors' votes (Constitution clause 19.7A). The votes of Directors in meetings, including the elections of the CEO, CFO and Women's Director, are open and are published. This does not apply to the votes of voters in an election.
9.39 A board officer who is elected to the Core Committee (Constitution clause 14.5). When the result of a seat is final, a Team Director, Deputy Director or Media Manager of a board who has won a Core seat leaves his board office. The board tells the General Secretary in writing within twenty-four hours. The board fills the vacancy within thirty days: a Team Director by election inside the board and, until then, the Deputy Director acts (Constitution clause 11.8); a Deputy Director by election among the twelve Voting Members (clause 11.3); a Media Manager by appointment of the Team Director and Deputy Director together (clause 11.4). The board reports the new holder to the General Secretary, and the board election complaint time of clause 11.6 applies. The person stays in the squad and keeps his vote as a Voting Member.

CHAPTER 10: MEETINGS, RECORDS AND PERSONAL DATA
10.1 Meetings are held on Google Meet. The notice gives the date, the time and the link, and the meeting starts at the time stated.
10.2 [Locked] A meeting that Constitution clause 19.7 says must be recorded is recorded, and the recording is kept for one year.
10.3 The General Secretary keeps the register of Member Boards, squads and officers. It shows the dates of joining and leaving, the order of each squad, the transfers, and the office holders.
10.4 [Locked] Minutes and records are kept always.
10.5 Only the officers who need personal details for their task may see them, for example the Election Committee during an election. Published squad lists show only names.
10.6 [Locked] Data of a player who has left:
    (a) These are always kept: display name, real name, board, dates of joining and leaving, records of the badge, price and fines, and the votes he cast.
    (b) The Gmail address, WhatsApp number and city are deleted within thirty days on the written request of the person.
    (c) If he does not ask, they are deleted automatically two years after he left.
    (d) Deletion does not take place while a price is owed to him, while a ban or sanction is in force, or while a complaint or appeal is pending.
    (e) These details are never shared outside IHCC (Constitution clause 10.4).
10.7 [Locked] The records of sanctions are kept as rule 8.36 says.
10.8 A tie among the Directors means the proposal is not accepted. The Chairman has no casting vote among the Directors. The proposal may be brought again the next week (Constitution clause 13.4A).
10.9 The Chairman presides over meetings of the Directors and does not vote. If he is concerned, the Deputy Chairman presides. If both are concerned or absent, the Directors choose one of themselves by more than half for that meeting only, and he keeps his own vote (Constitution clause 13.8).
10.10 The General Secretary calls the meeting on the Chairman's order with twenty-four hours' notice. If the Chairman does not call it, at least five Directors may demand it in writing, and the General Secretary calls it within seven days.

CHAPTER 11: FINANCE PROCEDURES
11.1 The monthly fee of a board is the real total cost of the month divided by the number of Member Boards of that month, rounded up to the next fifty Pakistani rupees for each board. The amount above the cost becomes credit. Boards of other countries pay the equal value. A new board pays from the month after it joins. A board that gives notice of leaving pays until the date of the notice. IHCC takes the fee only from boards, never from players. How a board collects it is its own affair.
11.2 The monthly report of the CFO under Constitution clause 22.5 is published in the Main Group on the first day of the month.
11.3 [Locked] The CFO keeps the record and the queue of the badge prices owed to players, and of the payments made under Constitution clause 9.8 and rule 2.14, with the leavers first and then the equal turns. The queue is public and shows only the display name and the queue number.
11.4 The money of fines is given in charity within [time to be decided], and the record under rule 7.9 is updated.
11.5 The money of IHCC is kept in the account of the CFO. The CFO gives a written statement that it is IHCC's and keeps a separate written register of every amount. Every spending or transfer needs the earlier written approval of the Chairman (in his absence, the Deputy Chairman). The Chairman receives the monthly bank statement; the General Secretary may see it; the Auditor compares it with the register at least twice a year. If the CFO is absent, the Chairman and the General Secretary choose an Acting CFO within two days.
11.6 [Locked] A price that IHCC cannot pay in full within the week of Constitution clause 9.9 gets a further week, and again each week, until it is paid. The CFO shows each such price and the number of weeks in the monthly report.
11.7 [Locked] The financial year runs from 3 December to 2 December. The first runs from 3 December 2026 to 2 December 2027. The Auditor checks the whole year in its last thirty days, from 3 November to 2 December. The CFO who ran the year prepares and signs the report of the year before 2 December or at the hand-over. The new CFO publishes it with the Auditor's report within thirty days after 2 December, that is by 2 January.
11.8 [Locked] The hand-over of the money. The CFO who leaves gives the new CFO in writing the amount of money, the debts, the queue of badge prices and the list of unpaid fees, and the General Secretary records it.
11.9 [Locked] The account of IHCC Management until 3 December 2026 is kept as Constitution clause 32.13 says. It shows each month the date, from whom, how much and for what. Three Directors chosen by lot, who have nothing to do with the Management, check it by 30 November 2026. At the hand-over the Management, the new CFO and the General Secretary sign a statement, and it is announced in the Main Group. It is a separate report and not part of the first financial year.
11.10 Spending: small, up to 2,000 Pakistani rupees at a time: the CFO and the Chairman in writing. Medium, 2,001 to 10,000: more than half of the Core Committee in writing. Large, above 10,000, or above 10,000 in a year for one purpose: the Board of Directors by more than half. Badge prices are a fixed debt and need only the CFO's record. In an emergency the Chairman may spend alone up to 2,000 and reports to the Directors within fourteen days. Every payment has a receipt or proof and a reason (Constitution clause 22.14).
11.11 A board that cannot pay a month's fee and gives a written reason may be given one month's delay, once, by more than half of the Directors. Only then do the months of Constitution clause 22.7 begin to count. A fee is never waived.
11.12 When a board leaves or is removed, its unpaid fees and debts are set off against its credit, and the rest is returned within thirty days to an account named in writing by an officer of the board, in his own name. The CFO makes a written account and the General Secretary records it. Credit is never kept as a sanction, and a fine is never set off against credit.
11.13 Sponsorship money goes only into the account of the CFO. Anyone else who receives it hands it to the CFO within three days and tells the General Secretary. The General Secretary keeps a written agreement for each sponsorship. A sponsorship of up to 10,000 Pakistani rupees is approved by the Chairman, or by the Deputy Chairman if the sponsor is the Chairman himself or his relative. A sponsorship of more than 10,000 is approved by the Board of Directors by more than half. The 10,000 is for one sponsorship, and not for the total of a year. The sponsor of a series between boards is approved by the General Secretary with the series (rule 5.2). A sponsor whose business is gambling, interest or alcohol is not accepted (Constitution clause 23.3). The sponsor gets only a public acknowledgment of the name. Money is bound to its purpose; if the purpose is cancelled, IHCC asks the sponsor and holds the money sixty days. A public record is kept every month. General sponsorship money may be used anywhere under rule 11.10. Money given for a particular series or purpose is used only for that.
11.14 Dissolution (Constitution Article 31). All badges stay in IHCC's account. Players already paid keep the price. Unpaid players are paid from what remains, in queue order. If the money runs out, the badge of an unpaid player goes back to him and the debt ends. The accounts are settled within ninety days: debts first, then the credit of the boards in equal shares, and unused sponsorship money back to the sponsors or in charity as the Directors decide. Website, groups, logo, email and records go as the Directors' last decision says.
11.15 At hand-over the new CFO takes the money into his own account in front of the General Secretary, with the written hand-over statement of rule 11.8.
11.16 Honorarium (Constitution clause 14.17). Each Core officer receives 1,000 Pakistani rupees each month, paid from the operational fee. The Board of Directors may change the amount by more than half, and the amounts are published.

CHAPTER 12: OFFICIAL COMMUNICATION
12.1 The official channels of IHCC are the Main Group, the official IHCC website, the IHCC WhatsApp channel, the IHCC YouTube channel and the official IHCC email address.
12.2 Official notifications are in plain text, numbered, and without lines or decoration, so that a screen reader reads them clearly.
12.3 Every notification gives the date, the subject, what is decided or asked, and by when.
12.4 Official notifications are in English. A translation may be added for understanding, as Constitution clause 4.2 says.
12.5 A wrong notification may be corrected within forty-eight hours, as Constitution clause 19.5 says.
12.6 Official announcements are made only by the General Secretary or the Chairman, as Constitution clause 21.1 says.

CHAPTER 13: TRANSITIONAL SCHEDULE
This Chapter holds the timetable of the first election and of the hand-over. It is to be removed from the Rule Book after the hand-over on 3 December 2026.
13.1 Timetable of the first election. All dates are in 2026 and all times are Pakistan time.
13.2 Saturday 31 October, 11:59 p.m.: squad lists close. Boards with fewer than twelve players are disqualified under Constitution clause 32.3.
13.3 Sunday 1 November: the disqualified boards are announced.
13.4 From 1 November to Tuesday 3 November: the internal election of every board under Constitution Article 11. By 3 November every board reports its election to IHCC under Constitution clause 32.5.
13.5 From Tuesday 3 November to Thursday 5 November: forty-eight hours for complaints about the internal elections under Constitution clause 11.6.
13.6 Thursday 5 November: the Team Directors are confirmed, and each Director names one person for the Election Committee, after asking him.
13.7 Friday 6 November: the Directors vote on these persons on Google Meet, and the Election Committee is formed. The Voting List is closed.
13.8 From Saturday 7 November to Tuesday 10 November: nominations for Chairman, Deputy Chairman and General Secretary. The campaign runs from the end of the nominations to Saturday 14 November.
13.9 Sunday 15 November, from 6 a.m. to 6 p.m.: voting. If fewer than 79 Voting Members vote, voting is held again on Sunday 22 November under Constitution clause 16.13, and the later dates move by the same number of days.
13.10 Sunday 15 November, evening: after the voting closes, the Head of the Election Committee announces the result and the total votes of each candidate, with the turnout and the names of those who voted. How each member voted is never published. For twenty-four hours, until Monday 16 November, evening, any member may say that a vote under his name was not cast by him.
13.11 From Tuesday 17 November to Thursday 19 November: nominations for CEO, CFO and Women's Director. Friday 20 November: the Directors vote on Google Meet, in a recorded meeting, and the result and how each Director voted are published.
13.12 From 1 November: the statements, the verification of display names and the final form under Constitution Article 9 are completed, board by board, within the dates that IHCC Management announces. Verification of names is not needed to put a Voting Member on the Voting List.
13.13 [Locked] The plan is that the Core Committee is complete by Sunday 22 November. If voting has to be held again, it is complete after that many more days, and in every case before 3 December. From then until Wednesday 2 December IHCC Management still runs IHCC (Constitution clause 32.8) and the elected officers prepare with it. Thursday 3 December: IHCC Management hands over, the Constitution and the Rule Book come into force, and the first terms begin and last until 2 December 2027 (Constitution clauses 32.1, 32.8 and 32.9).
